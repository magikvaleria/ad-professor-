## TL;DR — Key Takeaways

1. **Benchmark on Tier 1 only** — comparing worldwide traffic across funnels is apples to oranges.
2. **Don't over-scale winners.** When a small campaign works, leave it alone. Let it print quietly instead of burning it out chasing scale.
3. **Bottom-of-funnel ads should be ~1.5–3% of ad account spend, not 90%.** Adi's account was inverted.
4. **Top-of-funnel video (process ads from course footage) is the unlock** most people on the call are missing.

## Adi's Diagnosis (case study)

**Symptoms:** ~$2,500 spend, ~$1,500 gross, slight loss. $50 avg CPA on a $37 product. 35% get to the first button but only 6–8% click it.

**Root causes George identified:**
- **Ad account issue:** ~$2 clicks / high CPMs because 90% of budget was on **bottom-of-funnel** ads (offer written directly on the image). Meta was hunting purchasers in a small pool.
- **Sales page issues:**
  - Font too small
  - Male avatar creator — cat-buyers skew toward "cat lady" persona; swap the mood image
  - Module images too similar to each other
  - Black-on-black paper shown with a gray/black pen (should be white pen)
  - Bonus wolf portrait doesn't fit a cat buyer
- **Bigger issue than the page:** the ad account itself. Fix ads first, don't touch the page until spend is much higher.

**Prescription:**
1. Cut the BOF ads to $10/day, leave alone.
2. Build **top-of-funnel and middle-of-funnel video ads** — mostly process video from the course footage.
3. If you must run static TOF: put the offer *in the text*, not on the image. Force people to read.

## The "Don't Kill Your Winner" Playbook

George's big theme. At least 7–8 people in the group are printing $2–5k/mo profit off tiny campaigns they forgot about. Bryan on the call:

- 2 months live, ~$3.5k spend → ~$12k revenue, ~$4k/mo profit
- **Same 3 validation ads.** Never changed them.
- $60/day. One bump + one upsell (upsell was a course that failed as a front-end product).
- Bump was scoped in with the creator from day 1.

> "Bryan bought an apartment for half a million dollars and he's renting it out without paying the half a million." — George

**Rule:** When something works, don't touch it. Don't scale. Don't A/B. Every new creative you add cannibalizes the existing one. Add *different format* campaigns instead.

## Funnel-Step Framework

Terminology George uses interchangeably with awareness levels:

| Funnel step | Awareness | Example (Adi's cat course) |
|---|---|---|
| Top of funnel | Unaware | "Is everyone else bored after work?" (no product shown) |
| Middle of funnel | Problem-aware | Lady drawing a cat: "I can never get the whiskers right" |
| Bottom of funnel | Solution-aware | "Draw a cat that glows — 2 courses for the price of 1" (offer on image) |

**The scaling reality:** Bottom-of-funnel ads validate cheaply — that's why they work for $50 tests — but they refill the pool from a drip. To fill the pool you need top-of-funnel volume.

**Budget split George targets:** BOF ~1.5% of account (3% if he's "feeling wild"). Adi was at 90%.

## Ad Formats — The Framework George Is Building

A **format** = a completely different look and feel. If a viewer sees two of your ads, they shouldn't immediately recognize them as the same brand's ads. That's the test.

### Formats George is currently running

- **Promo video / short-VSL:** mostly process footage, "here's what's inside," CTA
- **UGC:** person talking, sometimes with creation in hand, sometimes without

### Bottom-of-funnel formats (he has plenty)

- Testimonials
- Grids of completions (3x3 finished-product grid works well)
- "Offer in your face"
- Same in image + video variants

### Formats George is looking for (the whole reason he called this meeting)

He's stuck at ~2 TOF formats. Wants ~5 so each can sustain its own low-budget campaign and he can move up-stream from ad-treadmill to business-development-treadmill.

### Ideas floated on the call

- **Meme / relatability ads** (Jamie's approach — see below)
- **Pixar / Claymation** — George's brand won't let him; group can
- **AI podcast** — creator's photo animated as podcast host (brand policy blocker for George)
- **Educational** ("5 common mistakes people make when sketching") — high on unaware scale
- **Slideshow-style** (Jasmine — used by gym apps; series of images with voiceover, TikTok/Reels-viral)
- **Song ad** — extreme, unproven
- **Split ads** — Pixar hook → real footage for the actual creation. Stephen's suggestion. Rule: the creation itself should not be AI, even if the wrapper is.
- **Testimonial funnel** built around one person's journey with the craft
- **Carousel step-by-step** (Peter) — probably counts as a format if it works
- **Poetic / diary-entry voiceover on process**

### What is NOT a new format

- Same photo shown as a still vs. a slow pan → same format, different edit
- Changing angle ("who can't" angle) → that's an angle, not a format
- Different persona in a UGC ad → still UGC

## Process-Video Ads (the main unlock)

**George's mandate to the group:** stop using stock footage. Start using footage from your own course.

Why it wins:
- Builds trust (real hands, real craft)
- Gives you filler space for voiceover, VSL text, ASMR
- Converts on this audience (crafters) where AI/stock reads as fake and kills the sale

**Jamie's process-video ad demo (her top earner, thousands and thousands of dollars):**

- Real footage from her workshop
- Little B-roll moments: ironing a seam, snipping a thread, folding fabric
- Signals "I could do this" — familiarity = accessibility
- Same ad copy across her account; **visuals are what she tests**
- She looks at Instagram Reels searches (e.g. "quilting") to see what the algo is rewarding, then builds ads that echo that rhythm
- No voiceover on her top ad — sometimes uses ASMR

Gabriel had been running AI-avatar/aspirational ads only. Committing to record process ads from his re-recorded course footage, launching Monday.

## Jamie's Meme / Engagement Loop (biggest single tactic surfaced on the call)

**How it works:**
1. Browse **adjacent niches** on Instagram (knitting, crochet, sewing for her)
2. Find memes that are getting share/comment traction
3. Convert them into a **niche-specific meme** ("we always buy more yarn" → "we always buy more fabric")
4. Overlay text on B-roll (or Instagram Remix if you're being lazy)
5. Run as **engagement ads**, not conversion — a few dollars a day is fine

**Result:**
- Wildly cheap engagement ($0.0001s per engagement in her account)
- CPMs on her conversion campaigns drop for days after a meme takes off
- Meta learns who her people are — signal she can't buy any other way
- Adds followers she can retarget

**Doesn't convert directly.** It feeds top-of-funnel. Retargeting picks up the engagers.

Jamie also runs a reach campaign at ~$1–2/day to build audiences for the brand — 12¢ CPMs.

**Also runs reach ads at her email list before launches** — everyone should do this. George does it too.

## Quiz Funnels

George launched a quiz funnel on the same product as his long-form sales page — same result as the new ad formats: lower CPM, lower CPA, higher volume. Not scaling it; parking it.

**About-the-same numbers as sales page** — same % of clicks reach the end of the quiz as reach the first CTA on the sales page. Quizzes aren't more expensive per outcome than George once believed. (He'll verify and report back next call.)

### How the quiz should work
- Linear (not branching). Answers don't route.
- Purpose: make it feel *about them*, not about you
- Personalize the result page (or fake the personalization) to their answers
- On the result page: **short e-com-style product page**, not long-form
- Above the fold: creation, price, guarantee, button. That's it. The quiz did the digging.
- Platform: HighLevel (Hayflow)

### What NOT to do
- Don't gate the offer behind a lead-magnet opt-in — closes the loop, kills the sale
- Don't collect emails from non-buyers unless you're running a Jamie-style email nurture (higher-ticket only)
- Peter proposed branching-by-objection → George reframed: that's a customer-service survey, not a quiz. Quiz should feel like *they* are learning about *themselves*.

### When branching makes sense
- Multiple products (Jamie segmenting subscribers into which module fits them)
- Higher-ticket ($500+) with real nurture email flow

## AI Ads — Brand Constraints

Crafters *hate* AI in the end product. Even an animated element in a hook triggered backlash for George's brand. Group agreement:

- Full-AI (Pixar / Claymation) — people accept because it's obviously stylized
- Realistic-AI — accepted more than
- **Half-AI / uncanny valley** — worst
- The **creation itself** should never be AI. That's the rule.

George's brand has stakeholder politics preventing him from going hard on Pixar-style — expects to win that internal fight in 6 months. Others in the group have no such constraint.

If you're getting "this looks AI, not buying" comments:
1. It probably looks AI. Change it.
2. Run counter-positioning ads: "not AI" and nostalgia ("do stuff with your hands like the 90s") are working angles.
